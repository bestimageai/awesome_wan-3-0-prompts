# People, dialogue and localization

Twelve briefs for people, dialogue and localization. Use consenting people and licensed voice references. Dialogue language is intentional and separate from the English visual direction. Reference audio is not a guarantee of voice cloning or exact dubbing. [Catalog](README.md) · [Capabilities](../guides/model-capabilities.md)

## 01｜Community radio hands over the weather segment

**Mode:** T2V · **Settings:** 18s, 16:9, sound on · **Inputs:** none

```text
Create an 18-second fictional community-radio exchange. The left adult host wears a dark green shirt; the right host wears a brick-red sweater. Two microphones sit on a wooden table; rain is visible beyond the studio glass. At 0–7s the left host says in English, “The rain is easing, but the paths are still wet.” At 7–10s both pause; the right host looks from the window back to the speaker. At 10–15s the right host says, “We will leave a little earlier, then.” Hold to 18s. Use a slow push and natural room tone. Preserve faces and seats. No actual forecast claim, subtitles, overlap, readable screens, labels, or listener lip movement.
```

**Adjust:** approved fictional lines, clothing. **Review:** only the assigned speaker moves their mouth; the concept still cannot prove lip sync.

## 02｜Support instructions followed by one visible action

**Mode:** T2V · **Settings:** 16s, 16:9, sound on · **Inputs:** none

```text
Create a fictional 16-second remote-support scene with an adult customer at a desk and a specialist visible on a tablet. At 0–5s the specialist says in Spanish, “Muéstrame la caja azul que está sobre la mesa.” The customer waits with hands still. At 5–10s the customer picks up the single blue box and asks, “¿Esta caja?” At 10–14s the specialist answers, “Sí, esa. Déjala cerrada, por favor.” Hold to 16s as the customer nods. Keep one medium camera, clean voices, and quiet room tone. Preserve box, tablet, faces, and hand ownership. No electrical connection, opening the box, subtitles, simultaneous speakers, private data, or unexplained success signal.
```

**Adjust:** object color and matching dialogue together. **Review:** instruction, action, and confirmation occur in that order.

## 04｜Voiceover while a craft worker stays silent

**Mode:** T2V · **Settings:** 15s, 16:9, sound on · **Inputs:** none

```text
Create a 15-second observational portrait of an elderly basket maker in a plain blue shirt, opening a workshop. At 0–5s they lift a folded cloth from a workbench. At 5–10s they arrange three willow strips. At 10–15s they begin one slow weave. The subject remains silent with a relaxed closed mouth. A separate off-camera adult narrator says in Japanese, “この工房の朝は、道具を並べるところから始まります。” Use one slow lateral camera move and quiet cloth and wood sounds beneath the narration. Preserve age, clothing, hands, strips, and bench. No voice assigned to the subject, sentimental music, subtitles, invented biography, or instant finished basket.
```

**Adjust:** craft materials and matching narration. **Review:** narration never causes the on-screen subject to lip-sync.

**Mode:** T2V · **Settings:** 16s, 16:9, sound on · **Inputs:** none

```text
Create a fictional 16-second community announcement with one adult presenter in a gray jacket and a blank blue wall. At 0–3s the presenter looks directly at the camera. At 3–12s they say in English, “A quick correction: our workshop starts at seven, not six. ## 08｜Two short sounds in a language lesson
```

**Mode:** T2V · **Settings:** 16s, 9:16, sound on · **Inputs:** none

```text
Create a 16-second English pronunciation lesson concept for adults. A teacher stands beside two blank cards, with the full face and both hands visible. At 0–5s the teacher points to the left card and says “cap,” then repeats it once slowly. At 5–10s they point to the right card and say “cup,” then repeat it once slowly. At 10–16s they say “cap, cup” once and pause for the learner. Use a fixed portrait camera and dry, clear audio without music. Preserve face, card positions, and hand ownership. No subtitles, distorted mouth, exaggerated accent caricature, extra words, student voice, or unreadable phonetic text.
```

**Adjust:** the minimal pair after teacher review. **Review:** a language instructor must assess the generated pronunciation; prompt text alone is not proof.

At 11–16s they ask, “咁我今日由邊一步開始呀？” At 16–20s the older cook replies, “先慢慢摺好個邊。” Use one two-shot, clear distinct voices, and soft bowl sounds. Preserve seating, faces, hands, and dough count.
